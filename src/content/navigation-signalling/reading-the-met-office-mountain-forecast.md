## Why it matters

Weather on the hills can be far harsher than in the valley below. The Met Office mountain forecast is written for ground at about 300 metres and above, and is the quickest way to see what a ridge or summit will feel like.

## When it is issued

The forecast is issued each afternoon before 6pm and refreshed before 6am. It covers the evening ahead, the next two full days, and a short outlook for days three to five.

## Reading the sections

- **Wind and gusts.** Shown at several heights between valley and summit, every three hours. The figures describe typical exposed ground, so ridges and tops can be windier.
- **Temperature and "feels like".** The second figure includes the cooling effect of wind and other conditions.
- **Freezing level.** The height above which the ground is expected to be frozen or snow to settle. It does not allow for patches melting in sunshine.
- **Visibility.** How clear the air is above low cloud, from very hazy to excellent.
- **Cloud on the hills.** The chance of cloud-free tops tells you whether to expect a view, and how long low cloud may linger.
- **Severe chill.** A hazard flagged when strong winds, usually a mean above 35 mph, greatly increase cooling.
- **Hazards.** Nine hazards are rated nil, low, medium or high. Treat the rating as a first look, then read the full forecast.
- **Meteorologist's view.** Notes local effects the tables can miss, such as wind funnelling along ridges or strong downslope gusts.
- **Confidence.** Explains what is uncertain. If a weather front may arrive earlier or later than forecast, plan to be off the hill well before it.

## A simple routine

1. Check the forecast the evening before and again in the morning.
2. Read the hazards, then the wind and "feels like" columns at your highest point.
3. Decide a turn-back time and a lower alternative route.
4. Tell someone your plan, and be willing to turn back if conditions get worse.

## Limits

Conditions vary a lot by place, height and hour. A forecast is guidance, not a guarantee. If you cannot judge the risk, choose a lower, shorter route.
