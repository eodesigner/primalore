## Purpose

A large power cut can last from a few hours to a few days and can affect heating, lighting, phones, water supply, cash machines and internet. This list helps you get ready and cope.

## Who it is for

UK households. The 105 number and the Priority Services Register are UK-specific.

## Before a power cut

- [ ] Save **105**, the free national number for reporting a power cut and getting updates, and write it on paper too
- [ ] A wind-up or battery torch, with spare batteries
- [ ] A battery or wind-up radio (an online radio needs wifi, so it will not work)
- [ ] A power bank, kept charged
- [ ] Bottled water; the Red Cross suggests three days' supply
- [ ] Long-life food that needs no cooking
- [ ] Warm clothes and blankets
- [ ] A little cash, as cash machines and card payments may fail
- [ ] A paper list of emergency contacts and essential medication
- [ ] Supplies for babies and pets if you have them
- [ ] If someone relies on medical equipment, join the free **Priority Services Register** through your energy supplier or network operator

## During a power cut

- [ ] Report the cut to your network operator on 105, and report any fallen power lines too; call 999 if there is a serious risk to people
- [ ] Keep fridge and freezer doors closed
- [ ] Switch off ovens, irons and electric fires, and unplug sensitive electronics; leave one light on to see when power returns
- [ ] Wear layers and use blankets; close curtains, windows and outside doors to keep heat in
- [ ] Save phone battery: lower the brightness, close apps, limit use
- [ ] Check on older or vulnerable neighbours if it is safe to do so
- [ ] Listen to a battery radio for updates

## Safety warnings

- **Never use camping stoves, barbecues or generators indoors.** They can give off deadly carbon monoxide.
- The Red Cross advises avoiding candles because of the fire risk. Use torches instead.

## After the power returns

- [ ] Switch appliances back on one at a time to avoid overloading
- [ ] Check food: if the cut lasted over about four hours, check refrigerated food carefully and throw away anything that has been warm for too long. A closed fridge stays cold for about four hours. A full freezer stays frozen for about 48 hours, and a half-full one for about 24. Throw away food that has defrosted for more than four hours. If in doubt, throw it out.

## When to review

Check the kit twice a year: batteries, charged power bank, water and food dates.
