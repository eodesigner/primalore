## Question

How do I tie a secure loop in the end of a rope, for example to anchor it to a post or tree?

## Answer

The bowline makes a fixed loop that holds under load and can still be untied afterwards.

1. Take the working end of the rope around your anchor (a tree or post), leaving a good length free.
2. Make a small loop in the standing part, the long part of the rope, with the working end lying on top of it.
3. Pass the working end up through that loop, behind the standing part, and back down through the same loop.
4. Hold the working end and the loop, and pull the standing part to tighten and "dress" the knot so the strands lie neatly.
5. Finish with an overhand knot around the standing part as a safety. Leave a tail of at least 10 cm (4 inches).

Check that the working end finishes inside the loop, not outside it.

## Caution

The Army guide rates the bowline at about 70 to 75 per cent of the rope's strength, and notes that knots can work loose over time, so check and re-tie them. Practise with a thick rope before relying on any knot. For anything that takes a person's weight, learn from a qualified instructor first.
